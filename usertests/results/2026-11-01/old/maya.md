# Usability test: Maya, new birder, phone

## T1. First look

**Answer:** It draws birds as little stick figures to scale so you can compare how big they are, with numbers for length, wingspan, and so on. First I'd tap "the birds around you now", or type a bird I know into "Find a bird to pin".

**Confidence:** 4

**Where:** The intro text at the top of the front page. The first bird tile (American Robin) is on screen too, but its drawing is a tiny green cross in a big empty box, so I couldn't tell what I was looking at until I read the text.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/

## T2. Merlin and Blue Jay

**Answer:** The Merlin is bigger, and the gap is mostly in the wings and the weight:

| | Merlin | Blue Jay | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 11.5 in | 10.7 in | Merlin about 0.8 in longer |
| Wingspan | 23.8 in | 15.2 in | Merlin about 8.6 in wider, roughly 1.5 times |
| Weight | 6.6 oz | 3.1 oz | Merlin about twice as heavy |

So my friend is right about the length but wrong about the rest. In the drawings the Merlin's wings are obviously much longer, which I liked.

**Confidence:** 4. The lengths have a little "~" after them and I didn't know why until later (it means estimated).

**Where:** I searched "Merlin", which only offered a "Pin Merlin" link. Then I put both birds side by side in the Pinned view.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/?find=Merlin, http://localhost:8081/?pin=merlin&pin=blujay&show=pinned

## T3. New York in October

**Answer:** Blue Jay (1 in 24 of the birds recorded), American Robin (1 in 35), and American Crow (1 in 36). Black-capped Chickadee comes next.

**Confidence:** 4

**Where:** I couldn't get the Month or Region buttons to open; tapping them did nothing for me. I ended up on the "more ideas" help page, which happens to have a "New York in October" link. Without that link I'd have given up on this one.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/ (tried opening Region and Month), http://localhost:8081/docs/using#some-things-to-try, http://localhost:8081/?region=NY&month=Oct

## T4. Three ducks

**Answer:** The **Mallard** has the widest wingspan (34.6 in, against 31.3 in for the Canvasback and 28.0 in for the Wood Duck). The **Canvasback** is the heaviest (2.6 lb, against 1.9 lb for the Mallard and 1.4 lb for the Wood Duck).

**Confidence:** 4

**Where:** I searched each name to get its "Pin" link, then looked at all three in the Pinned view. Searching "duck" just said "24 birds match. Type more to list them," which didn't help.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/?find=duck&show=pinned, http://localhost:8081/?find=Canvasback&show=pinned, http://localhost:8081/?find=Wood%20Duck&show=pinned, http://localhost:8081/?pin=mallar3&pin=wooduc&pin=canvas&show=pinned

## T5. Solid and dashed

**Answer:** Solid lines are measured and dashed lines are estimated. On the wing, the solid ends are the measured wing chord, and the dashed middle means the full wingspan is estimated.

**Confidence:** 5

**Where:** The "Using BirdView" help page (the "more ideas" link). The bird pages also say it in a paragraph under the drawing. Nothing on the front page tells you.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/docs/using#some-things-to-try, http://localhost:8081/species/shshaw

## T8. The wing chord

**Answer:** The wing chord is 18.4 in. In the drawing it's the solid part at each end of the long horizontal wing line, up to the little tick marks.

**Confidence:** 3 for where it is in the drawing, 5 for the number. The drawing has labels, but on my phone they're far too small to read; I could only just see a green word near the left end of the wing. I got where it is from the paragraph under the drawing and the number from the table. I still don't really know what a wing chord *is*, and the site never says.

**Where:** The Great Blue Heron's page: the paragraph under the drawing and the "Wing chord" row of the table.

**Pages:** http://localhost:8081/species/grbher3

## E1. What confused or annoyed you

The filter buttons (Month, Region, and the rest) wouldn't open for me, which was the most annoying part. I only found New York in October because the help page had a ready-made link. On the front page the drawings are tiny, just a small green cross in a big dark box, so the main thing the site is for doesn't show up on a phone until you pin a few birds. Search doesn't take you to the bird, it only offers "Pin", and typing "duck" just told me to type more. Words like "chord", "1 in 43 species observations", "Frequency Tier 1", and the "~" after numbers aren't explained where you see them. On the bird pages the name is cut off ("Great Blue Her..."), the labels inside the drawing are unreadable, and the sources column is cut off on the right. Each bird page has a long table full of "AVONET v7, sheet AVONET2_eBird" and "mass^(1/3)" that means nothing to me.

## E2. What you liked

Pinning two birds and seeing them side by side at the same scale really works. Seeing how much longer the Merlin's wings are than the Blue Jay's was a real "oh!" moment. The numbers are right there on each card, and the ready-made comparisons (small, medium, large, huge birds, and New York in October) are a nice shortcut. I liked that it tells you honestly which numbers are measured and which are estimated.

## E3. One change

Make the drawings big enough to see on a phone and make the search box take you straight to the bird, with a plain "compare" button to add another one.

## E4. Ease of use

**2 out of 5.** Comparing two birds was fun once I worked out the Pinned trick, but I needed the help page for almost everything else, and on the subway I wouldn't have read that far. I might come back to settle a "how big is it really" question, but not to browse.
