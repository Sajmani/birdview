BirdView is non-commercial. It is built on these freely available sources, with thanks:

  • Morphometric traits — Tobias, J. A. et al. (2022). AVONET: morphological, ecological and geographical data for all birds. Ecology Letters 25:581–597. doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.16586228, licensed CC BY 4.0.
  • Wingspans — Fox, M. R., Brunton, E. A. & Shephard, J. M. (2026). Avian wingspan database. doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.32089083, licensed CC BY 4.0. Only empirical wingspans are used; the rest are modeled.
  • Region boundaries — Made with Natural Earth, in the public domain. Used only by the Here button, in your browser.
  • Names, families, and species codes — AviList Core Team (2026). AviList: The Global Avian Checklist, v2025b. doi:10.2173/avilist.v2025b, licensed CC BY 4.0. English names default to eBird/Clements usage; AviList’s are an option.
  • Where each bird is found — occurrence records from GBIF: the EOD — eBird Observation Dataset, doi:10.15468/aomfnb, and the iNaturalist research-grade observations, both licensed CC BY 4.0. Only the derived list of regions per species is kept; no record, coordinate, date, or observer reaches this site.
  • Which species are in scope — BirdView's own, derived from the occurrence records above, not from a checklist: a species is drawn when its records recur in some region, in repeated episodes across years. Method and limits: how the data is made. Escaped cage, aviary, and farm birds also pass that rule; they are left off this page and listed with the reason for each.

Every length here is modeled, as are most wingspans. Click any bird to see where each number comes from. One diagram shows how the sources become the drawings.
