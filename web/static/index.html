BirdView

BirdView helps you understand the relative size of birds across families by providing abstract diagrams based on public and modeled data. Read the Instructions for details, and use the Controls to configure the view. Contact birdview at ajmani dot net with feedback.

Where the numbers come from

Solid lines are measured. Dashed lines are estimated. So a bill and a tail are drawn solid, a neck and a body dashed, and the head is left hollow — a dash means nothing on a closed shape, so an empty outline says it instead.

The wing crossbar is split. Its solid outer ends are the measured wing chord, from the bend of the wing to the longest primary; the tick marks show where that measurement reaches. Only the span between the ticks is estimated, and it is drawn dashed when it is — so for two thirds of these birds the wingtips are real and the middle is inferred.

A ~ after a measurement means the same thing in the numbers. Most lengths are estimated: no open dataset publishes body length for North American birds, so BirdView models it and says so.

Click any bird for every measurement, including each part of the diagram, with the source or the model behind it named individually.

The controls

Order by
Sort every bird by total length, wingspan or mass. Birds are then grouped into sections of similar size.
Scale
Per section rescales each section to itself, so every bird is big enough to read. This is the default. Global draws everything shown at one scale instead — a hummingbird really is a speck beside a swan. Global is the honest view across families; per section is the better one for comparing shape within a group.
Section step
How much larger a bird must be to start a new section. Smaller values give more, tighter sections.
Units
Inches and ounces, or centimeters and grams.
Family
Everything is selected when you arrive. Click a family to drop it, an order to drop the whole group, or All to clear the lot and start picking. Grouped by taxonomic order in checklist sequence — the order field guides use.
