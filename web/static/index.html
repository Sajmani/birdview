BirdView

How big is that bird, really? Every bird is drawn to one shared scale, so you can see the difference rather than read it off a number.

Instructions

The diagram

  • Bill — the exposed culmen
  • Head — cranium, estimated from bill depth
  • Neck — estimated per family; a drawing convention
  • Body — trunk, estimated from mass
  • Tail — dashed, to tell it from the body
  • Wingspan — the crossbar, at the same scale as the bird’s height, so a wing really is that long relative to the body

The controls

Order by
Sort every bird by total length, wingspan or mass. Birds are then grouped into sections of similar size.
Scale
Global draws everything shown at one scale — a hummingbird really is a speck beside a swan. Per section rescales each section to itself, which makes shape easier to compare within a group but hides the size gap between groups.
Section step
How much larger a bird must be to start a new section. Smaller values give more, tighter sections.
Units
Inches and ounces, or centimeters and grams.
Family
Everything is selected when you arrive. Click a family to drop it, an order to drop the whole group, or All to clear the lot and start picking. Grouped by taxonomic order in checklist sequence — the order field guides use.

Where the numbers come from

A ~ after a measurement means it is estimated rather than measured. Most lengths are: no open dataset publishes body length for North American birds, so BirdView models it and says so.

Click any bird for every measurement, including each part of the diagram, with the source or the model behind it named individually.
